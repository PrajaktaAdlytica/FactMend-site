# Factmend — scroll-story reference research

Research date: 8 October 2026. Discovery used the configured Perplexity MCP high research preset and ranked search; primary case-study pages were then read directly. This is reference research, not an implementation or live motion QA report. No current site in this document was visually exercised in a browser by this researcher. Mechanisms below are explicitly attributed to their makers' published accounts; a historical case study does not prove that a present homepage still uses the same mechanism.

The strongest additional references are **NITEX for chapter architecture, Vercel Ship for one pinned sequence, Resend for developer-oriented editorial pacing, and Daylight for an original, calm product narrative**. Zero Tech and Aether 1 are stretch references, useful for spatial continuity but too elaborate to adopt wholesale. These complement the user's Blackbird, Hear and Adaptyx references, which are being examined separately.

## Eight references

### 1. NITEX — narrative with an express route to action

- Website: [nitex.com](https://nitex.com/).
- First-party design account: [Spring/Summer on Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/10/10/nitex-building-a-brand-and-digital-platform-for-fashions-new-supply-chain/).
- Evidence: Its creators describe opening scroll-based chapters, expandable technical/content overlays with individual URLs, and a split between narrative exploration and direct action. Motion includes angled wipes and a viewport-tracking brand frame. The source confirms scroll storytelling, but does not establish continuous frame-by-frame scrubbing for every transition.
- Factmend adaptation: A concise record lifecycle story with optional **View provenance**, **Inspect destinations**, and **Read sample receipt** deep dives. Keep a direct **Map your memory deletion paths** route available from the top.
- Avoid/cost: Multiple modal-like overlays add focus, history and direct-link complexity. Prefer dedicated routes or a single accessible drawer rather than nesting overlays.
- Recommended fallback, not an observed feature: Stacked chapters on mobile; diagrams fully visible without animation; deep dives remain linkable ordinary pages. Do not copy its identity, angular logo motifs or fashion imagery.

### 2. Vercel Ship 2025 — a bounded pinned scene

- Reference URL: [Vercel Ship 2025](https://vercel.com/ship/2025).
- Primary source: [Vercel's build account](https://vercel.com/blog/designing-and-building-the-vercel-ship-conference-platform), 11 June 2025.
- Evidence: Vercel explicitly describes Motion `useTransform`, sticky containers, full-screen sections and a scroll-dependent zoom for its “What you can expect” section. This supports a scroll-controlled sequence. Its separate liquid hero experiments should not be called scroll-scrubbed without further verification.
- Factmend adaptation: One stable synthetic record remains in view while its provenance, correction and erase destinations change across three to five legible states.
- Avoid/cost: Do not copy its fluid/particle spectacle. Long pinned spans delay access to the portfolio and conversion. Limit the main pin to one essential explanatory chapter and provide chapter navigation.
- Recommended fallback: Unpin on narrow or short screens. Under reduced motion, show each complete state with its caption. The article does not establish a mobile or reduced-motion policy for this particular section.
- Availability limitation: An event page may have changed after the event; the published build account is the durable implementation reference.

### 3. Resend — technical storytelling without continuous spectacle

- Website: [resend.com](https://resend.com/).
- Primary source: [How we think about our website](https://resend.com/handbook/marketing/how-we-think-about-our-website), updated 8 July 2026.
- Evidence: Resend describes deliberate beginning–middle–end storytelling as visitors scroll, animation and interaction, and a rotating cube that expresses its values. It does **not** document that the cube is controlled by scrolling. Classify this as editorial sequencing plus motion, not verified scroll scrubbing.
- Factmend adaptation: Tell the buyer one specific operational story in plain language, then let technical detail increase progressively. Treat a clearly labeled memory record as the signature object.
- Avoid/cost: A borrowed abstract rotating object would recreate the generic aesthetic the user wants to avoid. Every visual must explain a fact's source, scope, version or destination.
- Recommended fallback: Ordinary semantic content remains complete, with optional motion removed. No need for WebGL to achieve this lesson.

### 4. Daylight Computer — a calm, specific narrative world

- Website: [daylightcomputer.com](https://daylightcomputer.com/).
- Primary source: [basement.studio's Daylight case study](https://basement.studio/post/elevating-daylights-gtm-strategy), 12 September 2024.
- Evidence: The studio describes a morning-to-night story, light-and-shadow changes, a soothing palette and smooth scrolling. This verifies narrative intent and smooth scroll, but does not establish precise scroll scrubbing or pin timing. The case documents the launch-era design.
- Factmend adaptation: Follow one record through its lifetime. Evolve the surrounding information as the fact is learned, corrected, expires and is erased. Premium can feel quiet and precise.
- Avoid/cost: Do not import lifestyle photography merely to soften enterprise software. Original diagram and interface craft should carry the story; photo/video asset production is unnecessary here.
- Recommended fallback: Keep the chronology as ordered still scenes on mobile; suppress environmental motion under reduced motion. Retain legible status and source labels throughout.

### 5. Forge Growth Infrastructure — expose the hidden layers

- Website: [forgegrowthinfra.com](https://www.forgegrowthinfra.com/).
- Primary source: [Slope's Forge case study](https://www.slope.agency/case-studies/forge-growth-infrastructure).
- Evidence: Slope explicitly describes parallax-driven layouts that reveal successive infrastructure layers, with a restrained system of lines, grids, geometric forms, technical renders and contextual data callouts. This is source-backed scroll depth/progression, not proof of a pinned or frame-scrubbed scene.
- Factmend adaptation: Reveal source → tenant/purpose boundary → index → cache → backup as distinct destinations with clearly different responsibilities. Use labeled layers rather than an unexplained node cloud.
- Avoid/cost: Do not borrow Forge's scale metrics or industrial imagery. Factmend has no verified operating metrics. Any sample counts must reconcile with visible fixture records.
- Recommended fallback: Vertical, labeled destination list with statuses. No depth/parallax on short screens or reduced-motion settings. Implement with crisp SVG/HTML before considering 3D.

### 6. Stripe Connect — visibility-triggered explanation

- Product URL: [stripe.com/connect](https://stripe.com/connect).
- Primary source: [Connect: behind the front-end experience](https://stripe.com/blog/connect-front-end-experience).
- Evidence: The documented Express onboarding animation starts when its element becomes fully visible and disconnects its observer. It is **visibility-triggered playback**, not scroll scrubbing. The historical build explicitly disables decorative animation for reduced-motion preferences.
- Factmend adaptation: A brief correction diff or retrieval-check result can play once upon entering view, while the main lifecycle chapter uses scroll progress. This reduces unnecessary pinning.
- Avoid/cost: Tiny animated dashboard text is unreadable. Show a selected field and a complete caption; keep essential output visible after playback. Historical CSS/browser guidance in this old engineering account must not be treated as current compatibility advice.
- Recommended fallback: Present the final result immediately; allow an optional replay when motion is enabled. The exact old animation may no longer appear on today's product page.

### 7. Zero Tech — coherent movement through a system

- Studio-hosted reference build: [Zero Tech](https://client-zero-tech.lusion.co/).
- Primary source: [Lusion's Zero Tech case study](https://lusion.co/projects/zero_tech/). Its **Launch Project** links to the studio-hosted build above, not necessarily the company's current homepage.
- Evidence: Lusion explicitly describes combining realtime 3D visuals with scroll navigation. It is an authoritative source for the mechanism, though exact frame control was not visually checked.
- Factmend adaptation: Preserve orientation as one record travels between clearly named destinations. A small path highlight can explain which copies are in scope for an operation.
- Avoid/cost: Full realtime 3D adds download, GPU, accessibility and maintenance demands. A trust-focused governance buyer needs readable evidence earlier than this style of immersive experience may provide.
- Recommended fallback: An ordered SVG or HTML map with complete labels. No camera flight or forced journey on mobile or reduced motion.

### 8. Aether 1 — a persistent object across chapters

- Reference experience: [aether1.ai](https://aether1.ai/).
- First-party technical account: [OFF+BRAND on Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/08/06/building-aether-1-sound-without-boundaries/).
- Evidence: Creators document baked 3D animation controlled by scroll progress with Three.js `AnimationMixer`, seven anchors, and an infinite looping experience. They also discuss complications with infinite scrolling and browser history. It is a fictional-earbuds proof of concept, not a live SaaS benchmark.
- Factmend adaptation: Only borrow continuity: the same record stays recognizable as the information and decisions around it change.
- Avoid/cost: Avoid infinite scrolling, magnetic/snap-like progression, sound, fluid cursors and a WebGL-only reading experience. This is the highest implementation-cost direction in the shortlist and provides little extra value for an evidence-heavy privacy workflow.
- Recommended fallback: Finite ordered chapters, each a complete still state with standard navigation. The authors report mobile performance work, but that is not an independent test and does not establish reduced-motion support.

## Recommended Factmend motion architecture

This is design synthesis, not a description of an existing product:

1. **Opening:** immediately show what Factmend proposes, for whom, and the labeled synthetic record. No blocking intro.
2. **Record learned:** provenance, tenant, purpose, consent and expiry attach to the fact.
3. **Record corrected:** show the old and new values with the source/version relationship. Old value remains legible as history, clearly distinguished from current retrieval.
4. **Erasure requested:** show each destination independently. An index and cache may clear in the simulation; backup status remains explicit and unverified.
5. **Result checked:** sample read-after-delete result plus a simulated receipt with scope, exclusions and backup caveats. Avoid animating “gone forever.”
6. **Portfolio and fit:** all five proposed modules have distinct names, clear responsibilities and real page links. Show existing memory-stack fit without claiming live integrations.
7. **Action:** offer a privacy-led sandbox assessment with a named acceptance-criteria conversation.

Use one bounded pinned sequence for steps 2–5 on suitable desktop viewports, with explicit state labels and predictable reverse scrolling. Everything else should use normal document flow, restrained entry motion and purposeful controls. Mobile gets a stacked four-step record timeline. Reduced-motion mode gets complete still states, not missing sections. The interactive demo must use deliberate controls for state transitions; scrolling the public narrative must not look like it is executing real corrections or erasure.

Do not claim that any reference's appearance proves conversion performance, accessibility compliance or loading speed. Those require separate testing. No third-party graphics, dashboard screenshots, claims or trademarks should be repurposed as Factmend assets.
